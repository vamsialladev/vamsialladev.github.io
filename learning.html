02 · Lessons I learned the hard way

Lessons I learned the hard way

  • Agreement isn't evidence.

    My LLM judge agreed with my 40 hand labels 95% of the time, and Cohen's kappa was 0.00: the labels held no failures to agree on, so no judge score counts yet.

    See the calibration →

  • A generous metric hides real misses.

    The note: my retrieval check passed on luck until I made it strict, and the honest score was lower.

    Read the note →

  • Measure the way users actually type.

    Testing the questions visitors type first took first-question success from 43% to 100% on the hotel assistant.

    See the results →

  • Cheaper can be just as good, if you measure it.

    On the same 54 questions, Haiku 5.5 matched Haiku 4.5 within run-to-run range at about 5× lower cost per 1,000 questions.

    See the comparison →

  • Check the grader before the model.

    Eight bugs in my own checks failed safe drafts, like “I'm not able to offer a free upgrade myself”. Every gate failure on the agent was a measurement bug.

    See what the eval taught me →

  • AI is one layer, not the whole system.

    Most of what makes an AI product work is ordinary good engineering.

    What I believe →
