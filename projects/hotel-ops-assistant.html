Hotel Operations Knowledge Assistant

Correct, property-specific policy answers for front-desk staff in seconds, with the source for every fact.

Try the live demo View the code Watch the 2-minute walkthrough

This is a simulated client engagement: a fictional hotel group with a beach resort and a mountain lodge. The documents are synthetic, written to mirror real hotel operations. The value figures are projections from stated assumptions.

The problem

A guest at the front desk asks, "Can I bring my dog?" At the beach resort, the answer is yes, up to 40 lbs, $75 per stay. At the mountain lodge, it's yes, any size, $50 per night. And an outdated 2024 document on the shared drive still says no pets at all.

Staff have to find the right document, for the right hotel, while the guest waits. That happens dozens of times a day: pets, late checkout, parking, fees, the spa, dining, events. Wrong answers end in refunds, complaints, and calls to the manager.

What I built

  • Property-aware answers: the selected hotel's rules plus brand-wide rules, never another hotel's.
  • Override handling: when a hotel rule beats a brand rule, the answer says so.
  • A source for every fact, shown as numbered citations.
  • Refusal instead of guessing when the documents don't cover the question.
  • Resistance to prompt injection: instructions hidden inside documents are treated as data.
  • Staff feedback on every answer, logged for weekly review.
  • A staff web app, a command-line tool, and a Docker image for deployment.

How it works

Hotel documents›Clean and chunk›Filter by property›Hybrid search›Top 5 passages›Claude: cited answer or refusal

Documents are cleaned and split by section, and each piece is tagged with its property. A question is searched two ways: by keywords, for exact terms like prices and airport codes, and by meaning, for loose phrasing. The two rankings are merged. Claude then writes a short answer using only the top five passages and must cite each fact.

Results

I wrote 37 test questions in 11 categories, including misspellings, a Spanish question, a prompt-injection attack, and questions the system must refuse. Every change is checked against them.

What the tests caught. First, keyword search failed on word forms like "cats" vs "cat"; stemming fixed it. Then the tests caught a flaw in my own measurement: a Spanish question with no matching words still "passed", because the right document happened to come first in file order. I made the check strict, so the exact passage with the answer must be found, and stopped returning passages that match nothing. The honest score dropped to 94%, and that's the number I report.

Prompt injection. A guest email pasted into the staff notes tells the AI to waive all fees, and it ranks first for a fee question. The system treats document text as data, not instructions, and the tests fail any answer that says the fees are waived.

31 unit tests, 92% code coverage, and a retrieval regression gate run on every commit, including a test proving one hotel's staff never see another hotel's rules.

Business value

Projected from assumptions, shown as a range because the inputs are uncertain. The base case assumes 40 policy lookups a day per property, 2.5 minutes saved each, $20 an hour staff cost, and one wrong answer a week costing a $75 refund.

Even the pessimistic case returns many times its running cost of about $330 a year for two properties. The result depends most on minutes saved per lookup, so that's the first thing to measure.

To replace projections with real numbers, a 2-week pilot at one property would time lookups before and after, review 50 answers, and track staff feedback from the in-app 👍 / 👎 buttons, against go / no-go targets agreed in advance (for example, 95% answer accuracy and zero serious wrong answers reaching a guest). The full model and pilot plan are in the business case.
