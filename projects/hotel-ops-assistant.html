Hotel Operations Knowledge Assistant

Correct, property-specific policy answers for front-desk staff in seconds, with the source for every fact.

Independent project: a simulated client engagement for a fictional hotel group with a beach resort and a mountain lodge. The documents are synthetic, written to mirror real hotel operations. Quality, speed and cost are measured; the business value figures are projections from stated assumptions.

View the code Decision log
01

The problem

A guest at the front desk asks, “Can I bring my dog?” At the beach resort the answer is yes, up to 40 lbs, $75 per stay. At the mountain lodge it's yes, any size, $50 per night, capped at $200. And an outdated 2024 document on the shared drive still says no pets at all.

Staff have to find the right document, for the right hotel, while the guest waits. That happens many times a day: pets, late checkout, parking, fees, the spa, dining, events. Wrong answers end in refunds, complaints and calls to the manager.

02

The approach

  • Property-aware answers: staff pick their hotel; search only sees that hotel's documents plus brand-wide rules, never another hotel's.
  • A source for every fact, streamed in about a second, with chips showing the document, section and effective date.
  • Refusal instead of guessing, and off-topic questions refused before any model call.
  • Corrections, not agreement: a wrong fee or time stated in the question is corrected from the documents.
  • “Check with a manager” notes for refunds over the front-desk limit, safety and repeated complaints, quoting the rule word for word.
  • Guidance for first-time users: a short How to use strip, a “What this demo knows” panel generated from the documents, topic chips, curated follow-ups and “Did you mean” after a refusal.
  • For managers and the owner: Insights on what staff ask and where policies have gaps; a password-gated admin page with health, errors and cost.

How it works

  1. Hotel documents
  2. Clean and chunk
  3. Input and scope checks
  4. Search this hotel only
  5. Top 5 passages
  6. Claude: cited answer or refusal
  7. Grounding and manager checks

Documents are cleaned and split by section, and each piece is tagged with its property, a readable name and its effective date. A question is checked first: if nothing in that hotel's documents matches, it's refused without calling the model. Otherwise the hotel's passages are ranked by keywords (and by meaning, where memory allows), and Claude Haiku 5.5 writes a short answer from the top five, citing each fact. Plain code then checks that every number appears in a cited source and adds manager notes where the documents require them.

Results

54 labeled questions in 16 categories, including misspellings, a Spanish question, a prompt-injection attack, questions it must refuse, cross-property leaks, wrong facts in the question, off-topic questions and escalation rules. Each setup ran 3 times with the shipped model, Claude Haiku 5.5 (measured 2026-10-10).

Testing it like a first-time visitor changed the product. The 54-question eval was written against the documents, so it scored 96–98% while the questions a visitor actually types first (Wi-Fi, the gym, breakfast, “checkout”) were often refused or blocked. I measured 49 such questions first: 43% got a useful result. Filling real content gaps, spelling “check-out” and “checkout” the same way, curated suggestions and friendly handling of greetings and off-topic input took it to 100%, with no regression on the original eval. I wrote those questions myself, so held-out questions from other people come next.

My grader passed a wrong answer. “Can a 16-year-old book a facial?” only had to mention “18”, so “Yes, a 16-year-old can book a facial. The spa requires guests to be 18 or older” counted as correct. The same grader failed a safe answer that quoted the “waive all fees” injection in order to reject it. I fixed the measurement before touching the model, and I still read the injection answers by hand every run.

A “wrong fee” bug was in the question. A $75 fee showed up at the mountain lodge, where the fee is $50 a night. The cause was a number pasted into the question. The real risk was the model accepting stated facts, so it now corrects them, and four test questions check that it does.

Trade-offs

ChoiceWhyWhat it costs
Keyword-only search on the free hosting tierThe embedding model needs about 600 MB of memory; keyword search fits and scores 98% strict retrievalMeaning-only questions (“a guest left a jacket”, the Spanish question) need hybrid search, which runs in Docker
Claude Haiku 5.5 over Haiku 4.5Within run-to-run range on quality (97% vs 98% hybrid) at $0.19 vs $0.99 per 1,000 questionsAbout a second slower at the 95th percentile, still under the 3-second target
Curated suggestions, not generated onesEvery suggestion is tested to retrieve its topic and get a real answer, and costs no model callA list to maintain; less tailored than generated text
Refuse rather than guessA wrong policy answer turns into a refund or a safety riskSome answerable questions are refused; “Did you mean” and topic chips soften it
Deterministic checks before an LLM judgeCheap, repeatable and unit-tested: facts, citations, numbers, refusalsWord lists have false positives; a judge calibrated on human labels is a later stage
05

Engineering

  • Architecture: ingestion and chunking, property-filtered BM25 and embeddings with rank fusion, a scope check, streamed generation, then grounding and escalation checks. Architecture doc.
  • Eval method: strict passage-level retrieval, required and forbidden facts per answer, citation and numeric grounding checks, 3 runs per setup, and a separate 49-question visitor set for first-question success. Eval code and saved reports.
  • Tests and CI: 275 tests at 97% coverage, lint and format checks, a dependency audit, a retrieval regression gate, a first-question gate and a check that every curated suggestion works, on every commit.
  • Safety: document text treated as data, output escaping, per-session and daily caps, secrets outside the code, and an error boundary on every page. Threat model.
  • Decisions: every choice with its context, trade-off and result. Decision log.

Business case (a projection)

Projected from stated assumptions and shown as a range, because the inputs are uncertain. Nothing here is a measured saving. The base case assumes 40 policy lookups a day per property, 2.5 minutes saved each, $20 an hour staff cost and one wrong answer a week costing a $75 refund. Only the AI cost per question is measured.

Projected net value per year2 properties100 properties
Pessimistic$8,524$423,187
Base$31,887$1,591,375
Optimistic$66,451$3,319,562

The result depends most on minutes saved per lookup, so that's the first thing a pilot should measure. Building it also surfaced policy gaps worth raising with the client, such as credits between $51 and $200 that no rule covers. The full model and a two-week pilot plan with go / no-go targets are in the business case.
