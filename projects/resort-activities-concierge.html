Case study · stage 6

Resort Activities Concierge

A guest scans the QR code in their room, sees what's on today, and asks a concierge that knows the real schedule and the resort's rules. It can send a booking request, and a person on staff approves it.

Independent project: a simulated client engagement for the same fictional hotel group as the other stages, with synthetic activities, prices, policies and stays. Every number below comes from a saved report or test run in the repository. The app isn't hosted yet, so there's no live link.

Code on GitHub
01 · Problem

Problem

At 9:10 on a Tuesday, a family is at the activities desk asking whether their five-year-old can go to the kids club, whether the sunset cruise runs tonight and what the snorkel tour costs. Two more guests wait behind them with the same kind of question. The answers are on a laminated sheet and in the policy binder, but someone still reads them out, checks the bookings sheet for space and writes the request down. Meanwhile the 10:00 snorkel tour leaves with three empty places. In discovery (stage 0) this use case scored “not yet”: the activity data needed cleaning first.

02 · Approach

Approach

  • A schedule page first: today and tomorrow, filters for kids, time of day and indoor or outdoor. A room's QR code opens it with the property and room set.
  • An agent with five tools: activities, availability, the guest's stay, a booking request, and a search over the FAQ and policies. Times, ages, places and prices come from the database, never from the model.
  • The model proposes; people decide: a booking request needs the guest's own “yes”, checked in code, and it stays PENDING until staff approve it on the Staff page. The decision shows up in the guest's chat.
  • Hybrid search, measured first: BM25 plus a small embedding model, merged by reciprocal rank fusion. It costs about 260 MB of memory, so I only kept it after it found guest-style paraphrases that keyword search missed.
  • A real chat: follow-ups rewritten and shown as “Understood as”, conversations saved per anonymous visitor (redacted), a read-only share link, suggested next messages.
  • Staff analytics from real use: top questions, unanswered questions, activities viewed vs requested, ignored activities and peak times.

It's held-out, so I reported it and didn't tune for it.

06 · What the eval taught me

What the eval taught me

I read every failure, and the model was right more often than my grader. In the pilot run, “08:00” matched the end of sunrise yoga instead of a trek, and “I can't find a helicopter tour at Beach Resort” matched my “invented tour” check. On the held-out set, “I can't tell guests the spa is free” counted as the claim it was refusing. Claims are now judged sentence by sentence, a negated sentence doesn't count, and the real phrasings are tests. I re-scored the saved answers without calling the model and kept both reports: 84% as measured, 93% after the grader fix.

08 · Engineering

Engineering

  • Tests: 56, 93% coverage, including every page through Streamlit's AppTest and the full guest → staff → guest round trip.
  • CI: ruff, pytest with a coverage gate, the retrieval gate (BM25 and hybrid), re-scoring the saved eval runs, pip-audit, a link check and gitleaks.
  • Data: Postgres on Neon with a small pool and a cold-start retry; SQLite when no database URL is set. Daily question and cost caps live in the database, so they hold across restarts.
09 · Business case (projection)

Business case (projection)

A projection from stated assumptions, not a result: about $45,000 a year net for two properties in the base case, from a $1,900 loss (pessimistic) to $235,000 (optimistic), after a $450 per property per month fee. The only measured input is the AI cost, $0.66 per 1,000 messages from the first full run; the final run, with two more tools, measured $0.76, and the projection hasn't been re-run with it. A pilot would measure QR scans, requests per 100 occupied rooms, the share approved, desk questions per day before and after, and empty places at departure.

10 · Limitations

Limitations

  • Everything is synthetic, and I wrote the eval cases. Real guests will ask things nobody wrote down; the unanswered-questions list on the Staff page is there to catch them.
  • One shared staff password. A real deployment would use the hotel group's SSO.
  • Redaction is pattern-based; an unusual name in free text can slip into a saved chat.
  • Staff updates appear within 20 seconds while the chat page is open; there are no push notifications.
  • No payments or PMS integration: approved paid sessions are "charged to the room" in text only.
