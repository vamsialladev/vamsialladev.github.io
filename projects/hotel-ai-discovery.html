Case study · stage 0

Discovery & Scoping

Before any code: six stakeholder sessions, six candidate use cases scored with a four-check test, a statement of work, a pricing model built on measured costs, and the security answers a procurement team asks for.

Independent project: a simulated client engagement. The people and their answers are fictional and written to match the synthetic documents of the later stages. It was written after stages 1–5 were built, so it explains their order rather than predicting it.

Read the discovery pack
01 · Problem

Problem

A hotel group wanted AI help at the front desk but hadn't agreed what to build first, what it could touch, or what it should cost. The front-desk lead described a guest waiting four minutes while they radioed a manager about one fee. The GM was clear: one confident wrong answer about money reaching a guest, and it gets switched off.

02 · Approach

Approach

  • Discovery: 10–15 questions each for the GM, front-desk lead, activities manager, revenue manager, IT/security and a guest, ending in users, success metrics, data sensitivity, constraints and open questions.
  • Problem selection: six use cases scored on value, feasibility, risk and data readiness, then a four-check test: someone has felt the problem, someone would pay, a first version is buildable in a week, the first user is known.
  • Scope and price: a statement of work with acceptance criteria per stage, and a pricing script whose only measured inputs are the model costs from the later stages' release runs.
  • Security: answers to an 18-question procurement questionnaire and a risk register, each pointing at what was actually built; anything not built is marked roadmap.
03 · What came out of it

What came out of it

The four-check test
Use caseResult
Policy knowledge assistantPass (built as stage 1)
Guest email agentPass (stage 2)
Tools with roles and audit (MCP)Pass as an enabler (stage 7)
Revenue questions + anomaly alertsPass (stage 8)
Guest activities conciergeNot yet: data needs cleaning, no property agreed to a guest pilot
Automatic refunds and upgradesFail: the GM would switch it off

Two rules set the order: staff-facing before guest-facing, and read-only before anything that writes.

04 · Pricing, from measured costs

Pricing, from measured costs

The pricing script uses two measured inputs: $0.18 per 1,000 questions (stage 1, v1.6.0 release run) and $1.24 per 1,000 emails (stage 2, v1.1.0). Everything else is an assumption, shown as pessimistic / base / optimistic. The lesson in the numbers: model tokens are a few dollars a month for two properties, so support time, not tokens, is what a price has to track. All prices are projections, not quotes.

05 · Engineering

Engineering

  • Docs that can't drift: the pricing tables are written by the script, and a test fails if the document and the script disagree.
  • Checks: 25 tests; a doc check for required documents, broken links and placeholders; CI with ruff, pip-audit, gitleaks, markdownlint and a link check.
